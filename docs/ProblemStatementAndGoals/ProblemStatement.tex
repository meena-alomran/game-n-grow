\section{Problem Statement}




This project addresses the need for accessible, individualized skill practice
that provides autistic learners with meaningful activities and gives caregivers
and educators interpretable information about their performance.

\subsection{Problem}



Autistic individuals differ in their strengths, communication styles, sensory
preferences, and support needs. These needs can also change over time.
Consequently, learning activities must accommodate individual differences rather
than assume that the same challenge, presentation, or assistance will suit every
learner. The World Health Organization identifies this diversity and the
importance of accessible support~\cite{whoAutism}.

For autistic learners seeking support with everyday routines, functional
communication, or remembering task information, useful practice must balance a
clear learning purpose with an understandable and engaging experience.
Caregivers and educators need to select relevant activities, adjust the
challenge and support provided, and account for the learner's preferences. An
activity's educational purpose alone does not establish whether an individual
can comfortably participate in it or wishes to repeat it.

Caregivers and educators also need to understand what changes in activity
performance mean. A higher score may reflect greater familiarity with a task,
additional prompting, or reduced difficulty. Without information about these
conditions, comparisons across sessions can be misleading. Research on the
planning-assessment game ShopAutiPlan illustrates the value of examining how a
task is completed alongside whether it is completed. However, interpreting game
measures as evidence of a broader ability requires validation specific to the
activity and intended population~\cite{shamhan2026}.

The problem addressed by this project is therefore the need to bring together
individually appropriate practice, voluntary engagement, and contextualized
performance information within a manageable caregiver-supported experience. The
intended deliverable is a unified suite of learning games that allows caregivers
and educators to configure practice and review a learner's activity history.

The initial scope comprises three activities addressing routine sequencing,
functional communication, and short-term memory. These activities will provide
opportunities to practise selected skills and observe performance under
documented conditions. The project will not diagnose autism, assign an overall
developmental level, or treat improved game performance as proof of improvement
outside the activities.
