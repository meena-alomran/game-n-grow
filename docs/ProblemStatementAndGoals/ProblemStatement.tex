\section{Goals}
The following five goals define the project's core benefits and the evidence
needed to demonstrate their achievement. Success will be evaluated through
software verification, content review, and formative evaluation with intended
users. These evaluations will establish whether the prototype meets its project
goals; they will not establish clinical effectiveness.

\subsection{Personalize practice to individual learning needs}

Enable caregivers and educators to select an appropriate challenge and amount of
assistance for each learner and activity. Independent configuration across
activities will accommodate differences in a learner's strengths and support
needs.

\textbf{Success criterion:} Each of the three core activities provides at least
three meaningful challenge levels and adjustable assistance. At least 80\% of
participating caregivers or educators can configure an activity for a specified
learner scenario without developer assistance following a brief introduction.

\subsection{Provide purposeful, repeatable skill practice}

Provide three complete activities addressing routine sequencing, functional
communication, and short-term memory. Each activity will offer repeated practice
toward a clearly defined objective, with feedback that helps the learner
understand the task and their response.

\textbf{Success criterion:} All three activities support a complete practice
session and have a documented connection between their learning objective, task,
feedback, and performance measures. Each activity's content and interpretation
limits will be reviewed by a relevant domain adviser, with identified concerns
addressed before final evaluation.


\subsection{Offer an engaging experience that respects learner choice}

Provide activities that intended learners find enjoyable and are willing to
participate in, while preserving their ability to pause, decline, or stop.
Engagement will be evaluated for each activity rather than inferred from
completion scores or time spent playing.

\textbf{Success criterion:} For each core activity, at least 80\% of
participating learners who try it provide a positive enjoyment response through
an accessible feedback method. Evaluation will also report voluntary
continuation or replay choices, early stopping, and unavailable feedback so that
the result does not conceal unsuccessful experiences.

Learner feedback and caregiver observations will be reported separately.
Findings will be limited to the participants and conditions evaluated.

\subsection{Make changes in performance understandable}

Provide caregivers and educators with accurate activity histories that support
decisions about whether to repeat, simplify, or advance practice. Results will
identify the challenge and assistance associated with each session so that
changes can be interpreted in context.

\textbf{Success criterion:} Reported results match expected outcomes across all
defined verification cases. At least 80\% of participating caregivers or
educators correctly interpret at least 80\% of representative progress examples,
including examples involving changes in difficulty or assistance.

The product will report performance within defined activities rather than
combine unrelated results into an overall developmental score.

\subsection{Support accessible and comfortable participation}

Allow learners to use the core activities through supported interaction methods
and sensory settings. Accessibility will address how learners access an
activity, complementing 2.1's focus on its learning challenge.

\textbf{Success criterion:} Every core activity can be completed through each
declared supported input method, with audio and nonessential motion disabled.
Learners can pause or end activities, and no essential task depends exclusively
on precise dragging, sound, or colour.

The interface will undergo review against applicable Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines criteria and cognitive-accessibility guidance, with the evaluated
scope and unresolved limitations documented. Familiar presentation and
configurable interfaces are supported by cognitive-accessibility guidance from
the World Wide Web Consortium~\cite{w3cPersonalization}.

\subsection*{Evaluation Basis}

The 80\% thresholds are proposed acceptance targets intended to require success
for a clear majority while exposing unsuccessful experiences. They are not
clinical benchmarks. Formative evaluation will aim to include at least five
caregivers or educators and at least five intended learners per activity;
participant counts and individual outcomes will be reported alongside
percentages.

Detailed procedures will be specified in the verification and validation plan.
Participation will depend on recruitment and applicable university requirements.
If intended learners cannot participate, expert review may support design
improvements, but the learner-engagement goal will remain unverified.

\section{Stretch Goals}
\subsection{Expand caregiver-controlled customization}

Allow caregivers and educators to further customize the visual
presentation of games to match individual learners' needs and
preferences. Options will include in-game brightness, colour palettes,
and the amount of decorative detail displayed. This extension will
provide more control than the basic accessibility settings included
in the core scope.

\textbf{Success criterion:} A caregiver or educator can adjust and save
these presentation preferences for an individual learner, and the
preferences are applied consistently across the core games. Supported
combinations preserve readable instructions and distinguishable
interactive elements.

\subsection{Enable multiplayer interaction}

Allow learners to participate in games with other players, creating
opportunities for shared play and interaction. Caregivers or educators
will be able to select multiplayer activities appropriate to the
learners' needs and preferences.

\textbf{Success criterion:} At least one game supports two or more
players in the same session, with each player's actions reflected in
the shared game state. Players can join, interact through the game's
supported actions, and leave the session. A supervising caregiver or
educator can manage participation and end the session.

\subsection{Expand the available practice areas}

Extend the suite with an additional activity addressing a learning
objective identified through stakeholder consultation. This extension
would broaden the available practice while preserving the consistency
of the learner and caregiver experience.

\textbf{Success criterion:} One additional activity satisfies the same
content-review, personalization, accessibility, and performance-reporting
criteria as the core activities. Its learning objective and interpretation
limits are documented, and its engagement results are evaluated
separately.
