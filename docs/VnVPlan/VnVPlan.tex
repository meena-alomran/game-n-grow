This document describes the plan for verifying and validating \progname{}, a suite of customizable learning games for autistic Learners and the CTMs who support them. The plan builds confidence that \progname{} satisfies the requirements in the SRS, and that those requirements reflect what Learners, CTMs and domain experts need. Section 2 summarizes the software under test, the objectives of verification and validation, and the documents this plan relies on. Section 3 describes the verification and validation team and the approaches used to verify the SRS, the design, this plan and the implementation, along with the automated tools and the validation activities. Section 4 specifies the system tests for the functional and nonfunctional requirements and traces each test to the requirements it covers. Section 5 will describe the unit tests once the detailed design is complete. The appendices give the values of the symbolic parameters used in the tests, and the usability survey.

\section{General Information}

\subsection{Summary}

Spectropica is a customizable educational game suite designed to help autistic
Learners practise skills through interactive activities. The initial games focus
on routine sequencing, functional communication, and short-term memory. Care
Team Members (CTMs), including parents, caregivers, and educators, can
personalize the games based on each Learner's needs, preferences, and abilities.

The system includes Learner profiles, configurable game settings, reusable
Templates, rewards, and a Dashboard for reviewing gameplay results. CTMs can
manage Learner profiles, assign games, adjust settings, and control access
permissions. Learners can participate in games using features such as
instructions, pause and restart controls, and rewards. Spectropica also supports
device pairing, gameplay data storage, and synchronization. The system
prioritizes iPadOS and Android tablets, with web access also planned.

Spectropica is intended to provide educational practice and activity-specific
performance information. It is not designed for clinical diagnosis or to
establish broader developmental improvements.

\subsection{Objectives}

The objective of the Verification and Validation (VnV) Plan is to build confidence that Spectropica meets its documented requirements and is suitable for its intended users. Verification will assess whether the system has been developed according to its specifications, while validation will evaluate whether it meets the needs of Learners and CTMs.

The VnV activities will prioritize the qualities identified as most important in the {\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/SRS-IEEE/SRS.pdf}{Software
Requirements Specification (SRS)}} and {\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/HazardAnalysis/HazardAnalysis.pdf}{Hazard Analysis}}. In particular, the Hazard Analysis prioritizes Learner wellbeing and privacy because failures in these areas could result in significant harm. As a result, particular attention will be given to preventing inappropriate sensory stimulation, unauthorized access to Learner information, incorrect gameplay configurations, and inaccurate or lost performance records.

The primary VnV objectives are outlined below.

\begin{description}

  \item[Learner Wellbeing, Accessibility, and Usability.] Establish confidence that Spectropica provides an accessible, understandable, and appropriate environment for Learners. Verification will examine sensory stimulation, gameplay initiation, flashing restrictions, error messages, and gameplay controls, as specified in SR-4 through SR-7 and SR-11. Validation will include CTM usability testing and Learner-focused evaluations. By default, the supervisor and special-education educators will play-test the games as proxies to evaluate sensory suitability, clarity of instructions, accessibility, and opportunities for independent participation. Direct testing with autistic Learners will only be conducted if recommended by the supervisor and the necessary ethics clearance and participant access are obtained. These activities will support evaluation of NFR-USE-1 through NFR-USE-5, with the limitations of proxy testing clearly documented.

  \item[Functional Correctness and Configuration.] Verify that the games, Learner profiles, Templates, rewards, and CTM permissions function according to the SRS. Testing will focus on ensuring that CTMs can correctly configure and assign games, that the selected settings are applied during gameplay, and that Learners can interact with the games as intended. Requirements-based testing, design reviews, and implementation checks will be used to identify inconsistencies between expected and actual system behaviour.

  \item[Privacy, Security, and Data Integrity.] Establish confidence that Learner information and gameplay records are protected against unauthorized access, loss, and corruption. Verification will examine CTM permissions (FR-TEAM-7), security requirements (NFR-SEC-1 through NFR-SEC-3), and the privacy and data-handling commitments in SRS Section 7.3. Particular attention will be given to incomplete-session handling, successful data saves, accurate exports, backups, and deletion procedures, as addressed by SR-8, SR-13, and SR-16 through SR-18. The system's implementation will also be reviewed against the applicable privacy and children's-app requirements identified in the SRS.

  \item[Reliability and Recovery.] Verify that Spectropica behaves predictably during interrupted gameplay, connectivity loss, and unsuccessful data operations. Testing will assess whether incomplete sessions are handled appropriately, authorized gameplay can continue during connectivity interruptions using available cached settings, and data is only reported as saved when the operation has been confirmed. These objectives are supported by SR-8, SR-9, and SR-13.

  \item[Educational Content Suitability.] Evaluate whether the content and difficulty levels of the games align with Ontario's Grades 3--5 elementary curriculum, as required by NFR-ACCU-2. Each game's content and difficulty levels will be mapped to relevant curriculum expectations and reviewed with the supervisor, with additional educator input where available. This will help establish that the games are appropriate for their intended educational purpose, rather than only confirming that they function correctly.

  \item[CTM Usability and Performance.] Evaluate whether CTMs can complete common tasks efficiently and understand the information presented by the system. Usability testing will involve at least 10 adults in CTM-like roles, such as parents, caregivers, educators, and special-education staff. Participants will complete representative tasks involving account creation, Learner setup, device pairing, CTM permissions, game configuration, and Dashboard interpretation. Their performance, feedback, and survey responses will be used to assess NFR-USE-1 through NFR-USE-4 and NFR-PERF-1. Repeated sessions will also help evaluate how easily CTMs remember how to use the system.

  \item[Stakeholder Validation.] Validate that Spectropica addresses its intended purpose through supervisor and domain expert feedback, prototype demonstrations, CTM usability sessions, and Learner-focused evaluations. Reviews with the supervisor will assess whether the system meets stakeholder expectations and identify requirements that need improvement. CTM testing will provide direct feedback on configuration, navigation, and progress tracking, while educator and expert proxy evaluations will assess Learner-facing gameplay. Direct Learner participation will remain conditional on supervisor guidance and ethics clearance. Findings from these activities will be documented and used to guide improvements to the system.

\end{description}

All requirements in the SRS are included within the planned academic-year scope, with no requirements intentionally deferred. However, the team recognizes that not all requirements can necessarily be supported by the same type of evidence. Design reviews will assess whether planned components and interfaces are appropriately specified, while implementation and system testing will assess their actual behaviour. Validation results will also distinguish between feedback from intended users and feedback from experts acting as proxies. In particular, proxy evaluations can identify potential usability concerns but cannot independently establish that the 80\% Learner participation target in NFR-USE-5 has been achieved. Any requirements that cannot be fully verified or validated will be identified along with the limitations of the available evidence.

\subsubsection*{Objectives Outside the Scope of VnV}

Although all SRS requirements remain within the project scope, the following broader objectives are excluded from VnV:

\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Clinical or long-term developmental effectiveness:} The team will not attempt to establish whether Spectropica produces clinical improvements or long-term developmental outcomes. Doing so would require specialized studies beyond the project's educational purpose and available resources.

  \item \textbf{Exhaustive verification of third-party software and infrastructure:} The team will not independently verify the internal correctness of external components such as the Godot engine, operating systems, or hosting services. However, Spectropica's integration with these components and its behaviour during relevant failures will still be tested.

  \item \textbf{Compatibility with unsupported platforms:} Verification will focus on the platforms and environments specified for Spectropica. Comprehensive compatibility testing across devices, operating systems, and browsers outside the supported scope is excluded due to the project's time and resource constraints.

\end{itemize}

These exclusions allow the team to focus its VnV efforts on the correctness, safety, privacy, usability, and educational suitability of Spectropica while remaining realistic about the limitations of an academic-year project.

\subsection{Custom Documents}

Two custom documents were approved for Spectropica, as identified in the
\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem
Statement and Goals}.

\textbf{Interview Report - Fall Semester:} This document will summarize
stakeholder interviews and feedback regarding the needs, preferences, and
expectations of Learners and CTMs. It will help the team better understand
accessibility considerations, gameplay expectations, and progress-tracking
needs. The findings will support validation of the project's requirements and
inform design improvements.

\textbf{Usability Evaluation Report - Winter Semester:} This document will
present the results of usability evaluations involving CTMs and Learner-focused
testing. It will examine how users interact with the prototype, including CTM
configuration tasks, Dashboard interpretation, and the accessibility and
suitability of Learner-facing games. The report will document observations,
participant feedback, identified usability concerns, improvements, and any
limitations of the evaluation methods.

Together, these documents will provide evidence that Spectropica has been
developed with consideration for its intended users and evaluated against their
needs.

\subsection{Relevant Documentation}

The following documents provide the requirements, design information, and
evaluation criteria used to guide Spectropica's verification and validation
activities.

\textbf{\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/SRS-IEEE/SRS.pdf}{Software
Requirements Specification (SRS)}:} The SRS serves as the primary reference for
verification by defining the system's functional, non-functional, safety,
security, and data requirements. These requirements establish the expected
behaviour and acceptance criteria against which the implementation will be
evaluated. They also provide the basis for requirements traceability and test
case development.

\textbf{\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/HazardAnalysis/HazardAnalysis.pdf}{Hazard
Analysis}:} The Hazard Analysis identifies potential failures that could affect
Learner wellbeing, privacy, system reliability, and data integrity. Its hazards,
safety requirements, and recommended mitigations help determine which areas
require the greatest attention during verification and validation. It also
provides a basis for developing safety-related tests and reviewing how the
system responds to potentially harmful conditions.

\textbf{\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem
Statement and Goals}:} This document describes the problem Spectropica aims to
address, its intended users, and the project's goals and success criteria. It
supports validation by establishing the purpose of the system and the user needs
that the completed software should address. It also identifies the approved
custom documents.

\textbf{\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/DevelopmentPlan/DevelopmentPlan.pdf}{Development
Plan}:} The Development Plan describes the team's development process, tools,
and implementation approach. It informs the selection of appropriate
verification activities, including code reviews, automated testing, integration
testing, and other quality assurance practices used throughout development.

\textbf{Module Guide (MG) and Module Interface Specification (MIS):} These
design documents will provide information about Spectropica's module structure,
responsibilities, interfaces, and interactions. They will support design
verification by allowing the team to review whether the proposed architecture
satisfies the requirements and whether module interfaces are defined
consistently. They will also inform module-level and integration testing.

\textbf{Interview Report:} The Interview Report will document stakeholder
feedback gathered during the Fall semester. Its findings will help the team
assess whether the system's requirements and design decisions reflect the needs
of Learners and CTMs.

\textbf{Usability Evaluation Report:} The Usability Evaluation Report will
document the results of the Winter semester usability activities, including CTM
testing and Learner-focused evaluations. It will provide evidence for assessing
usability requirements, identifying areas for improvement, and explaining any
limitations of the validation results.

Together, these documents will support a requirements-based VnV process by
connecting the project's goals, specifications, design decisions,
implementation, and evaluation results.

\subsection{SRS Verification}
\label{sec:srs-ver}

The SRS is verified in three ways: a checklist review by the team and classmate reviewers, a task-based review with the supervisor, and an automated traceability check. The reviews are applied to the submitted SRS, and their findings feed its next revision. Every finding is recorded as an issue labelled \texttt{srs-review} in the project repository, citing the affected requirement or section and the checklist item it fails. Accepted changes are recorded in the SRS revision history.

\paragraph{Checklist review.} The team and classmate reviewers review the SRS against the course SRS checklist. Each classmate reviewer files at least five issues. The following checklist items are given particular attention, because they apply directly to \progname{}'s SRS:
\begin{itemize}
  \item literal values are replaced by symbolic constants defined in the SRS appendix;
  \item nonfunctional requirements are verifiable, with fit criteria where appropriate;
  \item the SRS is consistent, unambiguous and traceable; and
  \item rationale is given for non-obvious requirements and scope decisions.
\end{itemize}
The reviewers also apply the project-specific checks in Table~\ref{tab:srs-checklist}, which cover parts of the SRS that the course checklist does not address directly.

\paragraph{Task-based review with the supervisor.} The supervisor reviews the SRS in a 60-minute meeting (\valref{1} in Section~\ref{sec:validation}). Rather than reading the SRS and giving general comments, the supervisor completes the following tasks with the SRS open, while a team member records their answers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=T\arabic*., nosep]
  \item Trace SR-LRN-1 to the functional requirements that support it, and judge whether they would protect a Learner with sensory hypersensitivity.
  \item Find the requirements that prevent a game from startling a Learner, such as sudden audio or flashing content.
  \item Review the default settings and reward approach, and judge whether they are appropriate for the intended Learners.
  \item Identify any requirement or wording that implies a diagnosis or a broader developmental conclusion.
  \item Identify any need of autistic Learners or CTMs that is missing from the stakeholder requirements.
\end{enumerate}
Each answer that points to a missing, incorrect or unclear requirement becomes an issue. The team then discusses with the supervisor which issues to resolve in the next revision of the SRS.

\paragraph{Traceability script.} A Python script in the project repository collects every requirement ID defined in the SRS and searches the SRS traceability matrices, the use cases, and the test cases and traceability tables of this plan for each one. It reports every requirement that is not referenced, and every test ID in a traceability table that has no matching test case. The script is run before each submission of the SRS and of this plan, and each gap it reports becomes an issue. The same script provides check P1 in Section~\ref{sec:plan-ver}.

\paragraph{Findings so far.} Writing the system tests in Section 4 was used as an early review of the SRS, and surfaced the following defects. These are filed as the first \texttt{srs-review} issues.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Consistency (S1):} UC8 allows any authorized CTM to delete a Learner profile, while \srsref{FR-LRN-4} allows only the Primary CTM.
  \item \textbf{Data dictionary (S2):} the outcome attribute of a Game Session has no value for incomplete sessions, which SR-8 requires.
  \item \textbf{Traceability (S3):} SR-LRN-2, SR-CUST-1, SR-CUST-2, SR-CTM-1 and SR-DOM-1 do not appear in the SRS traceability matrix, and the change log described in \srssectionref{sec:privacy-data-retention} has no corresponding functional requirement.
  \item \textbf{Symbolic constants:} the 30-day grace period and the percentages in NFR-USE-1 to NFR-USE-5 are literal values, and the backup retention period referenced by SR-18 is missing from the SRS appendix.
  \item \textbf{Verifiability:} NFR-EFF-1, NFR-RELY-1 and NFR-PERF-2 have no fit criteria.
  \item \textbf{Ambiguity:} FR-CTM-5 does not state who becomes the Primary CTM when a Primary CTM deletes their account, FR-PLAY-8 does not state where the CTM aborts a game from, FR-RWD-2 does not cover a Learner who already owns every reward item, and SR-12 does not state what happens to a hidden item that is already equipped.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Requirement-coverage check.} The traceability script from
Section~\ref{sec:srs-ver} is also run on this plan to list requirement IDs

\paragraph{Manual fault injection.} No mutation testing tool is assumed to be
available for GDScript, so once the tests are implemented the team will measure
test-suite strength by hand. A team member who did not write the code
introduces one small fault at a time in a separate branch and runs the full
suite. The faults target at least ten critical rules: the permission check
(\srsref{FR-TEAM-7}), the pairing-code retry limit (\srsref{FR-DEV-4}), the 30-day grace period
(\srsref{FR-LRN-6}), incomplete-session handling (\srsref{SR-8}), the hidden-item filter (\srsref{SR-12}),
the flash limit (\srsref{SR-6}), the export record count (\srsref{SR-16}), the low defaults
(\srsref{SR-4}), the invite-code expiry check (\srsref{FR-TEAM-1}), and the start-cue gate
(\srsref{SR-5}). The mutation score is the fraction of faults detected by at least one
failing test. Every surviving fault leads to a new or strengthened test.

\subsection{Implementation Verification}

\label{sec:impl-ver}

The implementation will be verified dynamically and statically.

\paragraph{Dynamic verification.} The system tests in Section 4 and the unit
tests in Section 5 are the primary evidence for functional and nonfunctional
requirements. Automated tests run in continuous integration on every pull
request (Section~\ref{sec:automated-tools}). Manual system tests are run by a team member
who did not implement the feature, before the Rev 0 demo and again before the
final demo.

\paragraph{Pull-request review.} Every pull request needs an approving review
from a member other than its author, following the rotation in
Section~\ref{sec:team}. The reviewer confirms that the change follows the
coding standard, uses static typing, includes or updates tests, contains no
credentials, and respects the relevant safety requirements. For example, no
audio or animation may start before the Learner's start cue (\srsref{SR-5}), and Learner
error messages must be calm and short (\srsref{SR-7}).

\paragraph{Code walkthroughs.} The team holds a 60-minute walkthrough for each
of these safety- or privacy-critical areas: access control and permissions,
pairing and invite codes with lockout, deletion and retention, offline queue
and synchronization, session recording, and data export. The author presents
the code, at least two other members inspect it against the requirements it
supports, and defects become issues. Areas that exist at Rev 0 are walked
through before the Rev 0 demo, and the rest before the final demo.

\paragraph{Static analysis.} Lint and format checks, strict typing warnings,
dependency scanning, and secret scanning run automatically
(Section~\ref{sec:automated-tools}). A pull request cannot be merged while these checks
fail.

\paragraph{Inspection of Learner-facing assets.} Screen captures are recorded to count flashes in every one-second window to check
\srsref{SR-6} (no more than three). The the default settings stored for each game are reviewed for low stimulation, volume, and celebration intensity (\srsref{SR-4}), and every task is checked so that it can be completed with touch or mouse input (\srsref{SR-10}).

\paragraph{Platform verification.} The manual platform matrix for
\srsref{NFR-PORT-1} to \srsref{NFR-PORT-3} is run on physical tablets owned by the team, and iOS and Android
simulators or emulators for older operating system versions, and Chrome,
Firefox, and Edge.

\paragraph{Out of scope.} The Godot engine and third-party libraries are
assumed to have been verified by their maintainers. The team tests only how
\progname{} uses them.

\subsection{Automated Testing and Verification Tools}
\label{sec:automated-tools} 


The team will use three automated tools for the Godot client, all of which run
from the command line so that they can be used locally and before a pull
request is merged. The commands are documented in the project
repository, and every team member runs them before submitting a pull request.
A change is ready to merge only when all tests pass, the linter reports no
errors, the formatter reports no differences, and Godot reports no warnings.

\paragraph{GUT (Godot Unit Test).} GUT is the unit testing framework for
GDScript. It is installed as an add-on in the project's \texttt{addons}
directory. Test scripts live in a \texttt{test} directory, extend
\texttt{GutTest}, and are named \texttt{test\_*.gd}. Each test function is
named \texttt{test\_<unit>\_<condition>\_<expected result>}, and a comment above
it gives the test ID from Sections 4 and 5 and the requirement ID it supports,
so that the traceability tables can be checked against the code. The team will use three GUT features in particular:
\begin{itemize}
  \item doubles and stubs replace the server connection, so that behaviour such
  as queuing results when the connection is lost (\srsref{FR-DATA-8}, \srsref{SR-9}) can be tested
  without a network;
  \item parameterized tests run the same check over several inputs, such as
  correct, incorrect, expired, and reused pairing and invite codes (\srsref{FR-DEV-4},
  \srsref{FR-TEAM-1}); and
  \item signal watching confirms that game state changes, such as pausing,
  restarting, and aborting, emit the expected signals (\srsref{FR-PLAY-5} to \srsref{FR-PLAY-7}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{gdlint and gdformat.} These two command-line tools come from
gdtoolkit, and the team will install the release that matches the Godot major
version used by the project. \texttt{gdlint} checks GDScript files against
naming, ordering, and design rules, with the team's chosen settings stored in a
\texttt{.gdlintrc} file in the repository. \texttt{gdformat} reformats files to a
single style. Team members run \texttt{gdformat} on the files they changed
before committing, and the pull-request check runs \texttt{gdformat --check}
and \texttt{gdlint} on the whole project. The check fails on any lint error or
any file that is not formatted. A consistent style keeps code reviews focused on
behaviour, and the lint rules catch problems such as unused or badly named
code before a reviewer has to.

\paragraph{GDScript warnings.} Godot's GDScript warnings are configured under
\emph{Project Settings} $\rightarrow$ \emph{Debug} $\rightarrow$
\emph{GDScript}, and the settings are saved in \texttt{project.godot}, so the
whole team shares them. Several useful warnings are ignored by default, so the
team enables them and turns on the option to treat warnings as errors. The
enabled warnings include untyped declarations, unsafe property access, unsafe
method access, unsafe casts, unused variables, shadowed variables, integer
division, and narrowing conversions. Together these make the team declare types
for variables and function signatures, which lets Godot find many type mistakes before the game runs.

\paragraph{Limits of these tools.} Passing these checks does not show that
\progname{} is correct. Lint, format, and warning checks find only style and
type problems, and GUT tests find only the faults they were written to detect.
The system tests in Section 4, the reviews and walkthroughs in
Section~\ref{sec:impl-ver}, and the fault-injection exercise in
Section~\ref{sec:plan-ver} supply the rest of the evidence.

\paragraph{\valref{2}: Rev 0 demo to the supervisor.} The team will demonstrate
\progname{} to the supervisor shortly after the scheduled Rev 0 demo. The
demonstration follows five scripted scenarios: (1)~a CTM registers (UC7), adds
a Learner, and pairs a tablet (UC2); (2)~the CTM personalizes settings (UC4)
and creates and assigns a Template (UC5); (3)~the Learner plays a game, using
pause and the guide, and claims a reward (UC1, UC6); (4)~the CTM reviews and
exports the Learner's data (UC3); and (5)~the Primary CTM invites another CTM
and edits their permissions (UC9, UC10). Afterward the supervisor completes a
feedback form that rates each stakeholder requirement in \srssectionref{sec:stakeholder-requirements} as
satisfied, partly satisfied, or not satisfied, with comments. Every
partial or unsatisfied rating is turned into an issue labelled \texttt{validation}, and
the team prioritizes these with the supervisor and resolves them before the
final demo. Changes to requirements are recorded in the
\href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/SRS-IEEE/SRS.pdf}{Software Requirements Specification (SRS)} revision history.

\paragraph{\valref{3}: CTM usability sessions.} At least 10 adults will be recruited
in roles close to the CTM personas, such as parents, educators, and
special-education staff, through the supervisor and the team's contacts. At least 10 are needed to be able to satisfy the 90\% target across \srsref{NFR-USE-1}--\srsref{NFR-USE-4}.
Each participant attempts these tasks while an observer records time, errors, and
comments: create an account, add and pair a Learner, invite a CTM, personalize a
game, and read the dashboard (UC7, UC2, UC9, UC4, and UC3, respectively). Participants then complete the survey in the
Appendix. A second session at least one day later repeats the tasks to measure
memorability (\srsref{NFR-USE-3}).

\paragraph{\valref{4}: Learner-facing validation.} Testing with children is research
involving human participants and would need ethics clearance, so the team will
not run sessions with Learners unless the supervisor advises it and clearance is
obtained in time. By default, the supervisor and special-education educators
play-test the games in UC1 and UC6 as proxies, using an observation guide that asks whether
anything would overstimulate or understimulate a Learner, whether instructions
are unambiguous, and whether a Learner could retry freely and feel a sense of
accomplishment. Their findings stand in for the Learner measurement in
\srsref{NFR-USE-5}, and the report of that measurement states that proxies were used.

\paragraph{\valref{5}: Curriculum alignment review.} A table is made that maps the content and each difficulty level of every game to Grade 3 to 5 curriculum
expectations from the Ontario Ministry of Education. The supervisor reviews the
table for appropriateness to the intended Learners (including the game and customization flows in UC1, UC4, and UC5), and educators outside the
team review it where available.

\paragraph{\valref{6}: Non-clinical wording review.} Before each demo, the team checks
user-visible text and dashboard labels to confirm that they describe
performance inside the games only and do not claim diagnosis or broader
developmental improvement (\srssectionref{sec_SystemProblem} and \srsnamedref{ULC_NonClinicalPurpose}{ULC Non-Clinical Purpose}). The supervisor reviews any
wording the team is unsure about.

\paragraph{Compliance check.} Each commitment in \srssectionref{sec:privacy-data-retention} is mapped to
the relevant PIPEDA and GDPR principles and to the app store children's-app
rules, and then verifies that the implementation provides the corresponding
behaviour.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] The application is on the CTM registration page. No account exists for \texttt{newctm@example.com}. A verified account already exists for \texttt{existingctm@example.com}.

  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-LRN-6} says that all Learner-related data is deleted after the grace period. \srsref{SR-18} requires the system to verify that the deletion has completed and to remove the data from backups. \srssectionref{sec:privacy-data-retention} specifies that permanent deletion includes all backups.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester advances the test server's clock past \texttt{GRACE\_PERIOD} and queries every table holding a Learner identifier for L1's identifier, confirming that no records are returned. The tester repeats the query on the latest backup, then logs in as L1's former Primary CTM to confirm that no option to restore L1 is shown.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Device Pairing}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-dev-1} test-fr-dev-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual

UC2 specifies that the Learner can see the correct Learner screen on their paired devices.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester enters the pairing code on T1 and confirms the pairing message on T1 and on CTM A's account. The tester generates a new code from CTM A's account, enters it on T2, and confirms the pairing message again. The tester then compares the Learner screens on T1 and T2 and checks that both devices are listed under L1.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-dev-2} test-fr-dev-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is the Primary CTM of Learner L1, and a pairing code for L1 is active. Tablet T1 is unpaired, with the application open on the pairing screen.
  \item[Input] An incorrect code is entered on T1 \texttt{PAIRING\_RETRY\_LIMIT} times, followed by the correct code. After \texttt{PAIRING\_LOCKOUT\_DURATION} has passed, the original code is entered again. CTM A then generates a new code, which is entered on T1.
  \item[Output] Once the retry limit is reached, further pairing attempts on T1 are blocked, and the correct code is refused. After the lockout period, the original code is still refused because it has been nullified. The new code pairs T1 successfully.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-DEV-4} says that the pairing method is nullified after a specific number of retries, and \srsref{FR-DEV-5} requires further attempts to be blocked for a period of time after the retry limit is reached. \srsref{FR-DEV-3} allows a CTM to generate a new code, which UC2 alternative flow 6.3 uses to recover from failed attempts.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester enters an incorrect code on T1 until the retry limit is reached, then enters the correct code and confirms that it is refused. The tester advances the test server's clock past \texttt{PAIRING\_LOCKOUT\_DURATION}, enters the original code again, and confirms that it is still refused. The tester generates a new code from CTM A's account, enters it on T1, and confirms that T1 is paired.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Care Team \& Permissions}

This testing area covers how CTMs join, are managed within, and leave a Learner's care team, as well as how permissions restrict what each CTM can do. The tests trace to the FR-TEAM requirements in Section 6 of the SRS.

\paragraph{Joining a Care Team}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-team-1} test-fr-team-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. CTM C has a verified account and is not part of L1's care team.
  \item[Input] CTM A chooses to add a CTM to L1's care team, selects permission to view L1's data only, and shares the generated invite code with CTM C. CTM C enters the invite code on their account.
  \item[Output] The system generates an invite code that expires after \texttt{INVITE\_EXPIRY}. CTM C is added to L1's care team with only the selected permission, L1 appears on CTM C's account, and CTM A is notified that CTM C has joined.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TEAM-1} says that the Primary CTM can add other CTMs to the Learner's care team via invite code. UC9 specifies that the invite code has an expiry time, that the new CTM is limited to the permissions selected by the Primary CTM, and that the Primary CTM is notified when the new CTM joins.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM A and generates an invite with the permission outlined in the input, then checks that the code's expiry is set to \texttt{INVITE\_EXPIRY} from now. The tester logs in as CTM C, enters the code, and confirms that L1 appears on the account. The tester checks that the care team membership record for CTM C holds only the selected permission, and confirms that CTM A received a notification.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-team-2} test-fr-team-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. CTM C is logged in and is not part of L1's care team. CTM D has already joined L1's care team using an earlier invite code.
  \item[Input] CTM C attempts to join L1's care team in each of the following cases separately:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item an incorrect code is entered \texttt{INVITE\_RETRY\_LIMIT} times, followed by the correct code
      \item the correct code is entered after \texttt{INVITE\_EXPIRY} has passed
      \item the code already used by CTM D is entered
      \item the correct code is entered after CTM A has cancelled the invite
    \end{enumerate}
  After each case, CTM A generates a new invite code, which CTM C enters.
  \item[Output] In every case, CTM C is not added to L1's care team and the system displays a reason for the failure. In case (a), further attempts are blocked for \texttt{INVITE\_LOCKOUT\_DURATION} once the retry limit is reached, and the correct code is refused. The newly generated code adds CTM C to L1's care team.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TEAM-1} says that CTMs join a care team via invite code, \srsref{FR-TEAM-2} allows the Primary CTM to cancel an outgoing invite, and \srsref{FR-TEAM-3} allows the Primary CTM to generate a new invite. UC9 alternative flows 7.1--7.3 specify that an incorrect, expired or used code is rejected, that retries are limited before further attempts are blocked, and that the Primary CTM can generate a new code.
  \item[Test Procedure] For each case (a)--(d), the tester sets up the code as outlined in the input, advancing the test server's clock past \texttt{INVITE\_EXPIRY} for case (b), and logs in as CTM C to enter it. The tester confirms that a failure reason is shown and that no membership record exists for CTM C on L1. The tester then generates a new code as CTM A, enters it as CTM C, and confirms that CTM C joins L1's care team. Before the next case, CTM C is removed from L1's care team.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Managing a Care Team}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-team-3} test-fr-team-3\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. CTM B is a member of L1's care team with permission to view L1's data and to personalize L1's settings, and is logged in on a separate device with L1's settings open.
  \item[Input] CTM A removes CTM B's permission to personalize settings and saves the change. CTM B then attempts to save a change to L1's settings. CTM A then chooses to remove CTM B from L1's care team, re-enters their password, and confirms the removal.
  \item[Output] After the permission change, CTM B's attempt to save is refused, and CTM B is notified that their permissions have changed. After the removal, L1 no longer appears on CTM B's account and CTM B is notified of the removal.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TEAM-4} says that the Primary CTM can edit the permissions of other CTMs, and UC10 specifies that new permissions are applied immediately and the affected CTM is notified. \srsref{FR-TEAM-5} says that the Primary CTM can remove another CTM from the care team, and \srsref{SR-3} requires the password to be re-entered before a CTM is removed.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM A on one device and as CTM B on another. The tester changes CTM B's permissions as CTM A, then attempts to save a setting as CTM B without reloading, and confirms that the save is refused and the setting is unchanged. The tester then removes CTM B as CTM A, and confirms on CTM B's device that L1 is no longer shown and that both notifications were received.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-team-4} test-fr-team-4\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. CTM B is a member of L1's care team.
  \item[Input] CTM B requests to transfer ownership of L1's profile to their account. CTM A first declines the request. CTM B then requests the transfer again, and CTM A accepts it and re-enters their password.
  \item[Output] When CTM A declines, ownership is unchanged. When CTM A accepts, CTM B becomes the Primary CTM of L1 and CTM A is no longer the Primary CTM. L1 has exactly one Primary CTM.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TEAM-6} says that a CTM can transfer ownership of a Learner's profile from another CTM, and UC2 alternative flows 1.1--1.2 specify that the current owner must confirm the transfer. \srsref{SR-3} requires the password to be re-entered before ownership is transferred. UC10 specifies that there is exactly one Primary CTM for a Learner.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM B and requests the transfer, then logs in as CTM A and declines it, confirming that CTM A is still shown as the Primary CTM. The tester repeats the request as CTM B and accepts it as CTM A with the password. The tester confirms that CTM B is shown as the Primary CTM and checks that only one care team membership record for L1 is marked as primary.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-team-5} test-fr-team-5\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. CTM B is a member of L1's care team with permission to view L1's data only. CTM C has a verified account and is not part of L1's care team.
  \item[Input] CTM B and CTM C each attempt the following actions on L1:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item change a personalized setting
      \item assign a template
      \item invite a CTM to the care team
      \item delete the profile
    \end{enumerate}
  CTM B attempts each action through the application, and CTM B and CTM C each attempt it by sending the request directly to the server.
  \item[Output] Every attempt is refused and L1's data is unchanged. CTM B can still view L1's data.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TEAM-7} says that a CTM cannot perform actions they do not explicitly have permission to do. \srsref{FR-LRN-4} and \srsref{FR-TEAM-1} also limit deleting a Learner's profile and inviting CTMs to the Primary CTM. Hiding an action in the application does not satisfy \srsref{FR-TEAM-7} on its own, so the server must also refuse the request.
  \item[Test Procedure] For each action (a)--(d), the tester logs in as CTM B and attempts the action through the application, then sends the same request directly to the server using CTM B's session, then using CTM C's session. The tester confirms that every attempt is refused and checks that L1's records in the database are unchanged. The tester then confirms that CTM B can still open L1's data.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Gameplay}

This testing area covers a Learner's experience during a game, from selecting a game in the game suite to completing or leaving it, including how the system behaves when the connection is lost or the game fails to load. The tests trace to the FR-PLAY requirements in Section 6 of the SRS, \srsref{FR-DATA-7} and \srsref{FR-DATA-8}, and the safety requirements \srsref{SR-5}, \srsref{SR-7}, \srsref{SR-8}, \srsref{SR-9} and \srsref{SR-11}. The tests follow UC1 and the gameplay states in Figure 2 of the SRS.

\paragraph{Playing a Game}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-play-1} test-fr-play-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1. L1 is authorized to play game G1 with personalized settings and is not authorized to play game G2. The application on T1 is on the game suite.
  \item[Input] The Learner selects G1, gives the start cue, completes every task, and exits to the game suite from the completion screen.
  \item[Output] The game suite shows G1 and does not show G2. G1 loads with L1's personalized settings and the completion screen is shown after the final task. The session is recorded as completed, with the settings used and the scores of each task.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-PLAY-1} says that a Learner cannot play games they are not explicitly authorized to play, and \srsref{FR-PERS-4} says that the personalized settings are loaded when a Learner plays a game. \srsref{FR-DATA-7} requires the Learner's performance in the session to be saved. UC1 specifies the flow from selecting a game to exiting it.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester confirms that only G1 appears in the game suite on T1, then plays G1 to completion as outlined in the input. The tester checks that the settings shown in the game match L1's personalized settings, then checks that the session record for L1 has a completed outcome, the settings used, and a score for each task.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-play-2} test-fr-play-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1 and is authorized to play game G1, with music and sound effects set above zero. The device volume on T1 is turned up.
  \item[Input] The Learner selects G1 and waits without giving the start cue. The Learner then gives the start cue and attempts to move to the next task before completing the current one, then completes the current task.
  \item[Output] Before the start cue, the game does not start and no audio or animation is played. After the start cue, the game begins. The next task is not presented while the current task is incomplete, and is presented once it is completed.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-PLAY-2} says that a game does not start until the Learner gives a cue that they are ready, and \srsref{SR-5} requires that no game audio or animation begins before this cue. \srsref{FR-PLAY-3} says that the next task is only presented once the current task is completed.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester records the screen and audio of T1 while selecting G1 and waiting without giving the start cue, then gives the cue and attempts to skip ahead before completing the first task. The tester reviews the recording to confirm that there is no audio or animation before the start cue, and confirms that the second task appeared only after the first was completed.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-play-3} test-fr-play-3\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1 and is playing game G1. CTM A, the Primary CTM of L1, has permitted the guide and playing again for G1.
  \item[Input] Each of the following is performed separately during a game of G1:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item the Learner pauses the game, then resumes it
      \item the Learner restarts the game
      \item the Learner aborts the game
      \item the Learner opens the guide for the current instruction, then closes it
      \item CTM A aborts the game from T1
      \item the Learner completes the game, chooses to play again, then completes it and returns to the game suite
      \item cases (d) and (f) are repeated after CTM A has disallowed the guide and playing again
    \end{enumerate}
  \item[Output] Pause, restart and exit controls are visible and responsive throughout. In case (a), the game stops and resumes on the same task. In case (b), the game starts again from the first task and the earlier session is recorded as restarted. In cases (c) and (e), the Learner returns to the game suite and the session is recorded as incomplete. In case (d), the guide for the current instruction is shown and closing it returns to the same task. In case (f), a new session starts and the Learner returns to the game suite afterward. In case (g), the guide and play again options are not available.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-PLAY-4} allows the Learner to read a guide if permitted by the CTM, \srsref{FR-PLAY-5} to \srsref{FR-PLAY-7} allow the Learner to pause, restart and abort a game, and \srsref{FR-PLAY-8} allows the CTM to abort a Learner's game. \srsref{FR-PLAY-9} allows the Learner to play again if permitted by the CTM, and \srsref{FR-PLAY-10} allows the Learner to return to the game suite. \srsref{SR-11} requires the pause, restart and exit controls to be visible and responsive at all times, and \srsref{SR-8} requires aborted sessions to be recorded as incomplete.
  \item[Test Procedure] For each case (a)--(g), the tester starts G1 on T1 and performs the actions outlined in the input, confirming that the pause, restart and exit controls remain visible and respond throughout. The tester confirms the behaviour described in the output for each case, then checks the outcome recorded for each session in the database.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-play-4} test-fr-play-4\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1 and is authorized to play game G1, with L1's settings already stored on T1. T1 is connected to the network.
  \item[Input] Each of the following is performed separately:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item T1's network connection is turned off after the start cue, the Learner completes G1, and the connection is turned back on
      \item T1's network connection is turned off before the application is opened, the Learner plays G1 to completion, and the connection is turned back on
      \item the application is force-closed during G1, then reopened with the network connected
    \end{enumerate}
  \item[Output] In cases (a) and (b), the game plays without interruption using the settings stored on T1. The session results are kept on T1 until the connection returns, are then uploaded and recorded as completed, and are removed from T1's local storage. In case (c), the session is recorded as incomplete.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{SR-9} requires that a loss of connection does not interrupt a session in progress or prevent a Learner from playing authorized games with settings already stored on the device. \srsref{FR-DATA-8} requires performance to be queued locally for future syncing without a stable connection, and \srssectionref{sec:privacy-data-retention} specifies that queued data is deleted from local storage after syncing. \srsref{SR-8} requires sessions that end through a crash to be recorded as incomplete.
  \item[Test Procedure] For cases (a) and (b), the tester plays G1 as outlined in the input and confirms that the game is not interrupted. While the connection is off, the tester checks that no new session record exists in the database. After reconnecting, the tester checks that the session record appears with a completed outcome and that T1's local queue is empty. For case (c), the tester force-closes the application, reopens it, and checks that the session is recorded as incomplete.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-play-5} test-fr-play-5\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]

  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1 and is authorized to play game G1. A test build is installed on T1 in which G1 is set to fail when loading.
  \item[Input] The Learner selects G1 from the game suite, then selects the action shown in the error message.
  \item[Output] The system displays a short, calm error message with no alarming sounds or visuals, offering a single action to return to the game suite. Selecting the action returns the Learner to the game suite.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{SR-7} requires error messages shown to a Learner to be calm and short, to avoid alarming sounds or visuals, and to offer a single action to return to the game suite. UC1 alternative flow 2.1 specifies that a game may fail to load with the correct settings.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester records the screen and audio of T1 while selecting G1. The tester confirms that one error message with a single action is shown, reviews the recording for alarming sounds or visuals, and selects the action to confirm that it returns to the game suite.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

This testing area covers how CTMs tailor games for a Learner, through game and difficulty authorizations, personalized settings, and templates. The tests trace to the FR-PERS and FR-TMPL requirements in Section 6 of the SRS.

\paragraph{Personalization}

\begin{enumerate}

  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-PERS-1} says that a CTM can toggle the authorization of specific games for a Learner, and \srsref{FR-PLAY-1} says that a Learner cannot play games they are not explicitly authorized to play. \srsref{FR-PERS-2} says that an authorized CTM can toggle the authorization of specific difficulties of a game for a Learner.

\item\label{test-fr-cfg-2} test-fr-cfg-2\\

The Learner then plays G1 on T1. CTM A then makes the server unreachable from their device, changes the stimulation level again, and saves.
  \item[Output] Before any changes, the stimulation level, volume levels and celebration intensity are set to their low default values.   \item[Derivation] \srsref{SR-4} requires the default stimulation level, volume and celebration intensity to be low until a CTM changes them. \srsref{FR-PERS-3} says that an authorized CTM can choose personalized settings of a game for a Learner, and \srsref{FR-PERS-4} says that these settings are loaded when the Learner plays the game. \srsref{SR-13} requires a save to be confirmed only after it has been verified, and the previous values to be kept if saving fails. UC4 specifies the settings that can be personalized.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM A, opens L1's settings for G1, and confirms that the stimulation level, volume levels and celebration intensity match the low defaults. The tester makes and saves the changes outlined in the input, plays G1 on T1, and checks that the settings recorded for the session match the saved settings. The tester then disconnects CTM A's device from the server, makes and saves another change, and confirms that a failure message is shown and that L1's stored settings are unchanged.

\paragraph{Templates}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-tmpl-1} test-fr-tmpl-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual

CTM A then assigns TP1 to L1 and L2, reviews the preview, confirms the assignment, and confirms that L2's existing template should be replaced. The Learner then plays G1 on T1.
  \item[Output] TP1 is saved in Folder 1 with the changed content, settings and difficulty. Before the assignment, the system shows a preview of TP1 and lists L1 and L2 along with what will be replaced for each, and requires confirmation. The system also asks whether to replace L2's existing template. After confirmation, TP1 is assigned to both Learners. The session on T1 uses TP1's content, settings and difficulty instead of L1's personalized settings.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TMPL-1} says that a CTM can create a template from an existing game, \srsref{FR-TMPL-3} to \srsref{FR-TMPL-5} allow the CTM to change the template's content, settings and difficulty, and \srsref{FR-TMPL-9} allows templates to be organized in folders. \srsref{FR-TMPL-6} says that assigning a template to a Learner overrides any previous settings, and \srsref{FR-PERS-4} says that personalized settings are not loaded when a template is assigned. \srsref{SR-14} requires the affected Learners, what will be replaced, and a preview to be shown before assignment, and UC5 alternative flow 6.1 specifies that the CTM is asked before an existing template is replaced.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM A and creates TP1 as outlined in the input, then confirms that TP1 appears in Folder 1 with the changes made. The tester assigns TP1 to L1 and L2, confirming that the preview, the list of affected Learners and the replacement prompt for L2 are shown before the assignment takes effect. The tester then plays G1 on T1, confirms that TP1's content and difficulty are presented, and checks that the session record references TP1 and the settings used match TP1's settings.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-tmpl-2} test-fr-tmpl-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learners L1 and L2. Template TP1 for game G1 is assigned to both Learners, and each Learner had different personalized settings for G1 before the assignment. L1 is paired to tablet T1 and L2 is paired to tablet T2.
  \item[Input] CTM A un-assigns TP1 from L1, reviews the preview, and confirms. The Learner then plays G1 on T1. CTM A then deletes TP1, reviews the preview, and confirms. The Learner then plays G1 on T2.
  \item[Output] Before each change, the system shows a preview of TP1, the affected Learners and what will be replaced, and requires confirmation. After the un-assignment, the session on T1 uses L1's previous personalized settings. After the deletion, TP1 no longer appears in CTM A's templates and the session on T2 uses L2's previous personalized settings.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TMPL-7} says that un-assigning a template from a Learner restores their previous settings, and \srsref{FR-TMPL-8} says that deleting a template un-assigns it from any associated Learners. \srsref{SR-14} requires the affected Learners, what will be replaced, and a preview to be shown before a template is un-assigned or deleted.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester records L1's and L2's personalized settings for G1 before the test. The tester un-assigns TP1 from L1 as CTM A, confirming that the preview and affected Learners are shown, then plays G1 on T1 and checks that the settings recorded for the session match L1's earlier settings exactly. The tester then deletes TP1 as CTM A in the same way, confirms that TP1 is no longer listed, plays G1 on T2, and checks that the settings recorded for the session match L2's earlier settings exactly.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-tmpl-3} test-fr-tmpl-3\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A owns template TP1. CTM C is logged in and has no templates. Two test files are prepared: a truncated template file, and a template file with a script placed in a content field that would write a marker file if executed.
  \item[Input] Each of the following is performed separately:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item CTM A exports TP1, and CTM C imports the exported file
      \item CTM C imports the truncated template file
      \item CTM C imports the template file containing a script
    \end{enumerate}
  \item[Output] In case (a), CTM C has a template with the same content, settings and difficulty as TP1. In cases (b) and (c), the import is rejected with a reason and no template is created. In case (c), the script is not executed.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-TMPL-2} says that a CTM can import a template from another CTM, and UC5 specifies that a template can be exported to a file. \srsref{SR-15} requires imported files to be treated as untrusted data and never executed.
  \item[Test Procedure] For case (a), the tester exports TP1 as CTM A, imports the file as CTM C, and compares the two templates' content, settings and difficulty. For cases (b) and (c), the tester imports each file as CTM C, confirms that a failure reason is shown, and checks that no template was added to CTM C's account. For case (c), the tester also checks that the marker file was not created on the device or the server.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Reward System}

This testing area covers how a Learner earns rewards from games and unlocks, equips and views cosmetic items, as well as how CTMs limit which items are available. The tests trace to the FR-RWD requirements in Section 6 of the SRS.

\paragraph{Earning Rewards}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-rwd-1} test-fr-rwd-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablet T1 and is authorized to play game G1. L1 owns every item in G1's reward pool except item I1.
  \item[Input] The Learner completes G1 and claims the reward. The Learner then starts G1 again and aborts it.
  \item[Output] After the completed game, the reward offered is I1, and claiming it adds I1 to L1's inventory as a game reward. After the aborted game, no reward is offered and L1's inventory is unchanged.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-RWD-1} says that a Learner can claim a reward after a completed game, and UC1 specifies that the reward is claimed after the completion screen. \srsref{FR-RWD-2} says that duplicate items are not offered as rewards, so the only item that can be offered is the one L1 does not own.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester plays G1 to completion on T1, confirms that I1 is the reward offered, and claims it. The tester checks that L1's inventory records contain I1 with a game reward source. The tester then starts G1 again, aborts it, and confirms that no reward is offered and that L1's inventory records are unchanged.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Cosmetic Items}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-rwd-2} test-fr-rwd-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 is paired to tablets T1 and T2 and has a known currency balance. Item I2 costs less than the balance, item I3 costs more than the balance, and item I4 is in the same category as I3 and costs less than the balance. L1 owns item I5, which is not equipped. The application on T1 is on the items screen.
  \item[Input] Each of the following is performed separately on T1:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
      \item the Learner buys I2
      \item the Learner equips I5
      \item the Learner attempts to unlock I3
      \item the Learner equips I2 while the server is unreachable
    \end{enumerate}
  \item[Output] In case (a), I2 is added to L1's inventory and the cost is deducted from the balance. In case (b), I5 is shown as equipped on T1, and also on T2. In case (c), I3 is not unlocked, and the system displays the reason, the conditions for unlocking I3, and I4 as an alternative. In case (d), the save fails, I5 remains equipped, and the Learner is informed with a calm, short message.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-RWD-3} allows a Learner to view cosmetic items, \srsref{FR-RWD-4} allows a Learner to unlock an item they meet the conditions for, and \srsref{FR-RWD-5} allows a Learner to equip an item they own. \srsref{FR-RWD-6} and \srsref{FR-RWD-7} require a reason, the unlock conditions, and buyable alternatives to be shown when an item cannot be unlocked. \srsref{SR-13} requires the previous values to be kept and the Learner informed if saving fails, and \srsref{SR-7} requires error messages shown to a Learner to be calm and short. UC6 specifies that currency is deducted when an item is bought.
  \item[Test Procedure] For each case (a)--(d), the tester performs the action on T1 as outlined in the input. For case (a), the tester checks L1's inventory records and balance. For case (b), the tester confirms that I5 is shown as equipped on T1 and on T2. For case (c), the tester confirms the reason, conditions and alternative shown. For case (d), the tester disconnects T1 from the server before equipping I2, confirms the message shown, and checks that I5 is still recorded as equipped.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-rwd-3} test-fr-rwd-3\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. L1 is paired to tablets T1 and T2, owns item I5 and has it equipped. L1 does not own item I6 and has enough currency to buy it.
  \item[Input] CTM A hides I5 and I6 from L1's items screen. The Learner then opens the items screen on T1 and on T2. Requests to buy I6 and to equip I5 are then sent directly to the server using L1's device session.
  \item[Output] I5 and I6 do not appear on the items screen on either tablet, and I5 is no longer equipped. The direct requests are refused, and L1's inventory and balance are unchanged.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{SR-12} requires cosmetic items hidden by a CTM to be hidden from the items screen and not to be purchasable or equippable by the Learner. UC6 specifies that the items screen applies any filters set by the CTM.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester hides I5 and I6 as CTM A, then opens the items screen on T1 and on T2 and confirms that neither item appears and that I5 is not shown as equipped. The tester sends the buy and equip requests directly to the server using L1's device session, confirms that both are refused, and checks that L1's inventory records and balance are unchanged.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Learner Data}

This testing area covers how CTMs view, filter and export a Learner's recorded game data, and how access to that data is restricted. The tests trace to the FR-DATA requirements in Section 6 of the SRS and to the safety requirements \srsref{SR-8} and \srsref{SR-16}.

\paragraph{Viewing and Exporting Data}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-fr-data-1} test-fr-data-1\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learner L1. L1 has a seeded history of 10 sessions of game G1 and 4 sessions of game G2 between October 1 and October 20. The G1 sessions include sessions on October 6 and October 15, and two aborted sessions. The expected averages and statistics are computed by hand from the seeded scores before the test.
  \item[Input] CTM A opens L1's data and expands the game history. CTM A then filters the history to October 6 -- October 15, opens the data for G1, and switches between each available data representation.
  \item[Output] The main page shows L1's most recent sessions and the average performance for each game, matching the hand-computed values. The expanded history lists all 14 sessions. The filtered history lists exactly the sessions between October 6 and October 15, including those on both dates. The G1 data shows L1's statistics and session history, and every representation shows the same values. The aborted sessions appear in the history as incomplete and are not counted in the averages.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-DATA-1} allows an authorized CTM to view a Learner's general data, \srsref{FR-DATA-2} requires an expandable overview of the game history, and \srsref{FR-DATA-3} allows the history to be viewed between specific dates. \srsref{FR-DATA-4} and \srsref{FR-DATA-5} allow game-specific data to be viewed in multiple representations. \srsref{SR-8} requires incomplete sessions not to be counted in a Learner's performance averages. UC3 specifies the flow for viewing a Learner's data.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester loads the seeded history and logs in as CTM A. The tester compares the main page, the expanded history and the filtered history against the seeded sessions and the hand-computed values. The tester then opens the G1 data, switches between each representation, and confirms that each one shows the same values and that the aborted sessions are marked incomplete and left out of the averages.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-data-2} test-fr-data-2\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] CTM A is logged in and is the Primary CTM of Learner L1, which has the seeded history from test-data-1.
  \item[Input] CTM A selects G1 and the dates October 6 -- October 15 on the dashboard, then exports L1's data for this selection.
  \item[Output] The export contains every G1 session record for L1 between October 6 and October 15 and no other records. The record count reported by the export matches the count shown on the dashboard for the same selection.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-DATA-6} allows an authorized CTM to export a Learner's game data. \srsref{SR-16} requires an export to contain every record for the selected Learner, game and date range, and to report a record count that matches the dashboard for the same selection.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester notes the record count shown on the dashboard for the selection, then exports the data. The tester opens the exported file, compares its records against the seeded G1 sessions between October 6 and October 15, and confirms that the reported count matches the dashboard count.
\end{description}

\vspace{0.05cm}

\item\label{test-fr-data-3} test-fr-data-3\\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=4cm, labelindent=0pt, labelwidth=!, style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries, before=\raggedright]
  \item[Control] Manual
  \item[Initial State] Learner L1 has the seeded history from test-data-1. CTM C is logged in and is not part of L1's care team.
  \item[Input] CTM C looks for L1 on their account. Requests to view and export L1's data are then sent directly to the server using CTM C's session and L1's identifier.
  \item[Output] L1 does not appear on CTM C's account. The direct requests are refused and no Learner data is returned.
  \item[Derivation] \srsref{FR-DATA-1} and \srsref{FR-DATA-6} limit viewing and exporting a Learner's data to authorized CTMs, and \srsref{FR-TEAM-7} says that a CTM cannot perform actions they do not explicitly have permission to do.
  \item[Test Procedure] The tester logs in as CTM C and confirms that L1 does not appear on the account. The tester then sends the view and export requests directly to the server using CTM C's session, and confirms that both are refused and that the responses contain none of L1's data.
\end{description}

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Tests for Nonfunctional Requirements}

The following subsections map to the different categories of nonfunctional requirements in \href{https://github.com/meena-alomran/game-n-grow/blob/main/pdfs/SRS-IEEE/SRS.pdf}{section 8 of the SRS}, with some combined with each other due to overlap in testing strategies.
Note that the carrying out of tests requiring a Learner's participation will be left to the discretion of the project supervisor and may be omitted if there are ethical or safety concerns.

\subsubsection{Usability and Performance}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-nfr-use-1} test-nfr-use-1\\

Type: Functional, Manual, Dynamic
					
Initial State: Software is running on a compatible device, with no account logged in.
					
Input/Condition: At least 10 individuals in the age range of a potential CTM (20-50) go through the account setup process, inputting their information and proceeding through the steps. Participants complete a usability survey afterwards.
					
Output/Result: Accounts are created with the individuals' information. At least 80\% of participants report an average 8/10 ease of use and satisfaction with performance.
					
How test will be performed: In-person sessions will be scheduled with participants. The developers will facilitate the test with their personal device and administer the survey afterwards on the same device through a Google form.

\item\label{test-nfr-use-2} test-nfr-use-2\\

Type: Functional, Manual, Dynamic
					
Initial State: Software is running on a compatible device, with a sample CTM account signed in and a sample Learner device paired.
					
Input/Condition: At least 10 individuals in the age range of a potential CTM (20-50) are given a series of actions to complete on the software such as navigating to a view or changing a setting. Participants will be asked to do each action three times total. Participants will complete a usability survey afterwards.
					
Output/Result: The participants are able to complete the specified actions. There is a 50\% increase of speed in completing these actions when doing so for the third time. At least 80\% of participants report an average of 8/10 or more ease in doing and learning these actions.
					
How test will be performed: In-person sessions will be scheduled with participants. The developers will facilitate the test with their personal devices, time how long the participants take to complete actions, and administer the survey afterwards on the same device through a Google form.

\item\label{test-nfr-use-3} test-nfr-use-3\\

Type: Functional, Manual, Dynamic
					
Initial State: Software is running on a compatible device, with a sample CTM account signed in and a sample Learner device paired.
					
Input/Condition: At least 10 CTM's for an autistic child (referred to as the Learner) in the intended range of 8-10 facilitates playing one game. Both CTM and Learner complete a usability survey afterwards.
					
Output/Result: The game is completed. At least 80\% of CTM participants report an average of 8/10 ease in facilitating gameplay. At least 80\% of Learner participants report an average of 8/10 or more ease of participating in gameplay (the CTM may answer on their behalf).
					
How test will be performed: In-person sessions will be scheduled with participants. The developers will facilitate the test with their personal devices and administer the surveys afterwards on the same device through Google forms.

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Accuracy and Efficiency}

\begin{enumerate}

\item\label{test-nfr-acc-1} test-nfr-acc-1\\

Type: Functional, Manual, Dynamic

Initial State: Software is running on a compatible device, with a sample CTM account signed in and a sample Learner device paired.
					
Input/Condition: 1-2 developers will simulate gameplay data for a chosen game, recording the sessions.

Output/Result: The data view for the game contains these sessions' data. The data is visualized in this view across at least 2 metrics. Observations of trends made by 2 developers who did not participate in simulating data are aligned with the gameplay sessions.
					
How test will be performed: 3-4 developers will carry out the test in-person using 2 of their personal devices (one for the CTM account and one for the Learner device). The developers will spend 15 minutes reviewing the data to measure whether data is indeed quickly understood through visualizations.

\item\label{test-nfr-acc-2} test-nfr-acc-2\\

Type: Non-functional, Manual, Static

Initial State: The contents of the games implemented within the scope of capstone are summarized in a document by the developers.

How test will be performed: 5 educators in Ontario who have taught or are currently teaching students in intended grade target of 3-5 will review the document outlined above within a week.

Result: The participants will confirm that the games' educational aspects are aligned with Ontario's curriculum for grades 3-5.
 
\end{enumerate}

\section{Appendix --- Reflection}

The following responses reflect on preparing the verification and validation
(VnV) Plan and the skills needed to carry out the planned activities.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}

One aspect that went well while working on the VnV Plan was completing the
deliverable ahead of schedule. This gave our team additional time to peer review
each other's sections, provide feedback, and make revisions before submission.
Having this extra time allowed us to identify areas that needed clarification
and ensure that the different sections were consistent with one another. Another
positive aspect was the opportunity to meet with our supervisor and domain
expert, who provided valuable feedback that we were able to incorporate into the
deliverable. Her insight helped us better understand the needs of our intended
users and consider important factors when developing our verification and
validation plans. Overall, completing the deliverable early and incorporating
feedback from both our team members and supervisor helped us strengthen the
quality of our work.

\item \textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and
how did you resolve them?}

One of the main challenges we encountered was deciding which testing methods and
test cases would be most appropriate for Spectropica. Since our system includes
several different features and user interactions, it was difficult to determine
how to evaluate everything thoroughly while keeping our testing plans realistic
and achievable within the project timeline.

To address this, we focused on identifying the most important features of our
system and prioritizing the testing activities associated with them. We also
considered the feedback provided by our supervisor to help guide our decisions.
This process helped us recognize that an effective VnV Plan is not simply about
including as many tests as possible, but about selecting meaningful tests that
address the system's requirements and most important risks.

\item \textbf{What knowledge and skills will the team collectively need to
acquire to successfully complete the verification and validation of the
project?}

\textbf{Amirah --- Test Planning and Requirements-Based Verification}

One skill I would like to further develop is designing effective test cases and
selecting appropriate verification methods based on software requirements.
Although I have experience working with software requirements and testing
concepts, applying them to a larger project with multiple interconnected
features requires additional consideration. Developing this skill will help me
identify which requirements need particular attention, establish clear expected
outcomes, and ensure that our testing activities provide meaningful evidence of
whether Spectropica meets its intended objectives.

\textbf{Maya --- Usability Test Design}

I would like to improve my ability to turn usability requirements into clear
tasks and measurable results. Spectropica's SRS includes goals such as CTMs
being able to configure Learner profiles with little difficulty, but evaluating
this requires defining what counts as difficulty and how it will be recorded.
Learning to design task-based usability evaluations would help me choose
appropriate observations and questions, avoid leading participants, and
distinguish problems with the interface from differences in users' experience.

\textbf{Meena --- System and Integration Testing}

Although I have a lot of experience from both university and co-op in writing unit tests and doing smoke testing,
I haven't done a lot of larger-scale tests like integration tests and system tests. It will be new to have to test,
both manually and through automation, that the larger components fit together. I also usually did not have to think
about usability in my co-op. I was always just carrying out the specifications made by the UI/UX team. I will have to think
critically about this aspect, especially while designing for children.

\textbf{Marko --- Godot Specific Testing}

I already have experience and knowledge with Godot's systems, but as for testing and validation I have only used basic debugging tools and relying on players to catch bugs. I have yet to work on something that requires this much care in regards to the amount of important requirements and things that require validation.


\textbf{Omar --- Full Stack Testing}

I have experience building full stack web applications, but I have never used a game engine to export or import data and interact with databases in real time. Debugging a frontend-backend issue has been a skill I've developed over time, but across a game engine with highly sensitive information, it is definitely a new level of security and privacy management I will acquire over the course of the project.


\item \textbf{For each knowledge area or skill identified in Question 3, what
are at least two ways to acquire it? Which approach will each team member
pursue, and why?}

\textbf{Amirah --- Test Planning and Requirements-Based Verification}

Two ways I could further develop this skill are reviewing software testing
resources and examples of requirements-based test plans, or gaining hands-on

My preferred approach would be to work directly with our project's requirements

described in the SRS. This would allow me to apply testing concepts to our

testing methods. I believe this approach would be the most beneficial because it
