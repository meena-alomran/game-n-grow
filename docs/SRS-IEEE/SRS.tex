\section{Introduction} 
% =======================================================

\subsection{Purpose}

The purpose of \progname{} is providing a range of games for caregivers, parents or educators to evaluate and develop the skills of autistic learners. The former may tailor the stimulation, difficulty or contents of the games to the needs of the child. 

\progname{} is intended to solve the lack of a engaging digital platform for autistic kids to develop their skills. Additionally, we aim to solve the problem that few tools exist for the comprehensive evaluation and progress tracking of autistic learners.

Moreover, physical and digital games designed for autistic kids tend to be rigid and difficult to customize for each student. We aim to solve this issue by allowing caregivers, educators and parents the ability to tailor the games in a variety of ways. 

\subsection{Scope} \label{sec_Scope}

The scope of our project is the development of a digital game suite for the evaluation and improvement of the skills of autistic learners under the jurisdiction of caregivers, parents and educators. The intended users are therefore autistic learners with varying skills in varying domains, and Care Team members who utilize features designed to tailor and support the learning path of the child.

The major functionality revolves around the core skill games, and the customization surrounding them. For example, the learning path of the child may involve a vocabulary-based game. The Care Team may customize the game through saving templates to: include words relevant to the child, adjust the colors and stimulative aspects of the game depending on child preferences, change the difficulty based on the child's progress, or decide the reward threshold for cosmetics. 

Expected benefits for a player perspective include the boosted engagement of autistic learners in a learning context, the enhancement of their progress in a diversity of skills. From a Care Team perspective, the expected benefits involve the identification of patterns or insights into the child's progress or skill profile through visualized data and numerical metrics that may have been difficult to track beforehand. Additionally, a digitalized platform is ideal for a Care Team member to quickly adapt games to different learners without creating or assembling games from scratch.

To further clarify the scope, \progname{} is intended to be used as a tool for Care Team members rather than a replacement for professional expertise. Within the timeline of Capstone, the software will not retain functionality for Care Team members to create their own brand new game types, design of their own tailored maps for games, or designing cosmetic rewards from scratch. The software will also not include a competitive component for players to compete against each other's scores.

\subsection{Stakeholders}
\begin{itemize}
\item Autistic Learners are a primary stakeholder and an end user group. They play the games and their skill growth is the main objective of the product. Accessibility, engagement and comfort are the priorities for this stakeholder group.
\item Customers are secondary stakeholders whoencompass anybody that makes the decision to acquire the software for autistic learners. This includes learning centers adopting the platform for students, parents or caregivers that purchase the software for their child.
\item Care Team Members are another end user group that are primary stakeholders. They are the ones familiar with the autistic learner. Consequently, they use both administrative privileges to customize games and the dashboard to read or analyze student-specific data. They are the main decision makers for the autistic learner's learning path and progress, and therefore consulted by the development team for the latter to prioritize the most important tailoring features.
\item Domain Experts are the final stakeholder group, falling into the secondary category. They are consulted by the development team to ensure the designed software is appropriate for autistic learners; aiding their progress and steering clear of any unintended harm. 
\end{itemize}

\section{System Overview}

This section provides an overview of \progname{}, including the problem it
addresses, its intended purpose, the environment in which it operates, its
users, and the constraints affecting its design. The overview establishes the
context for the detailed requirements presented in the following sections.

\subsection{Problem Statement} \label{sec_SystemProblem}

Autistic Learners have different strengths, communication styles, sensory
preferences, and support needs~\citep{nimhAutism2025}. These differences mean
that learning activities
suitable for one Learner may not be appropriate for another. Caregivers and
educators therefore need ways to provide activities that accommodate individual
needs rather than relying on a single difficulty level, presentation style, or
learning approach.

Providing personalized activities also introduces challenges in managing each
Learner's experience. When multiple individuals are involved in supporting a
Learner, they need a consistent way to manage learning activities, share
relevant information, and coordinate responsibilities. Without a centralized
system, maintaining individual configurations and understanding which activities
are appropriate for each Learner can become difficult.

Another challenge is interpreting performance over time. A Learner's results may
vary depending on the Game, its difficulty, and the conditions under which it
was completed. Without organized records of previous Game Sessions, Care Team
Members (CTMs) may struggle to identify patterns or make informed decisions
about future activities.

\progname{} addresses these challenges through a unified suite of customizable
learning Games supported by Learner profiles, CTM-managed configurations, and
performance tracking. The system allows CTMs to manage individual Learners,
collaborate through care teams, customize Games using Templates, and review
recorded performance information. Learners can participate in authorized Games,
receive feedback and rewards, and control their participation during gameplay.

\progname{} is intended to provide individualized practice opportunities and
support CTMs in understanding performance within the application. It is not
intended to provide clinical diagnoses or establish that changes in Game
performance represent broader developmental improvements.

\subsection{Product Vision} \label{sec_ProductVision}

\progname{} aims to provide autistic Learners with an engaging and personalized
environment for practising selected skills while giving CTMs a centralized way
to manage learning activities and review progress.

The system has five primary objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Support Individualized Learning:} Allow CTMs to manage
Learner-specific Game authorizations, difficulty levels, personalized settings,
and Templates so that activities can reflect individual needs and preferences.
\item \textbf{Enable Coordinated Care Team Management:} Provide a structured way
for multiple CTMs to support the same Learner while maintaining appropriate
permissions and responsibilities. A Primary CTM manages access to the Learner's
information and configuration.
\item \textbf{Provide Accessible and Engaging Gameplay:} Offer Games with
understandable instructions, opportunities for repeated practice, and rewards
intended to encourage engagement. Learners retain the ability to pause, restart,
or end activities.
\item \textbf{Support Meaningful Performance Tracking:} Record Game Session
results and provide authorized CTMs with access to individual Game histories,
performance representations, and data exports.
\item \textbf{Maintain Consistent Learner Experiences:} Allow Learners to access
their profiles through paired devices while preserving their assigned Games,
configurations, and associated information.
\end{enumerate}

The initial learning areas are routine sequencing, functional communication, and
short-term memory. These areas establish the intended focus of the initial
Games, while Templates allow CTMs to customize existing Games rather than create
entirely new Game types.

These objectives define \progname{}'s intended benefits without prescribing the
implementation of individual features. Together, they support a learning
environment in which CTMs can personalize activities, coordinate
responsibilities, and review information while Learners participate in Games
suited to their needs.

\subsection{System Context} \label{sec_SystemContext}

\progname{} is intended for use in environments where autistic Learners
participate in learning activities with varying levels of support from
caregivers or educators. These environments may include homes and learning
centres.

The system interacts with two primary user groups: Learners and CTMs. Learners
access the Game Suite to participate in authorized Games, while CTMs use the
Dashboard to manage Learner profiles, Game configurations, Templates, care team
permissions, and performance information.

Users interact with \progname{} through supported devices, with tablet
applications for iPadOS and Android forming the primary intended operating
environments. Web access is also planned. The system is being developed using
Godot, whose documentation describes exports for Android, iOS, and web
browsers~\citep{godotExportDocumentation}. Support for a particular platform
depends on the team's ability to implement and test it.

Devices provide the input, display, and connectivity capabilities needed to
access \progname{}. The system manages the association between Learners and
their paired devices.

The system boundary includes \progname{}'s application functionality running on
supported devices and its server-side functionality responsible for managing
accounts, permissions, configurations, Games, and records. The devices,
operating systems, browsers, and network connectivity used to access \progname{}
form part of its operating environment rather than separate application
components.

The system manages information associated with CTM accounts, Learner profiles,
care team memberships, Games, Game Sessions, Templates, and rewards. These
entities are internal to \progname{} and are described in
Section~7.

\paragraph{Learner Responsibilities}

Learners provide Game selections, responses to activities, and participation
choices. They may also interact with rewards and cosmetic items made available
through the Game Suite. Learners are responsible for making selections during
gameplay but are not expected to manage their own configurations or permissions.

\paragraph{CTM Responsibilities}

CTMs provide information needed to create and manage Learner profiles, configure
Games, customize Templates, and interpret performance results. Authorized CTMs
may manage Learner information according to their assigned permissions. The
Primary CTM is responsible for managing the membership and permissions of other
CTMs associated with a Learner.

CTMs are responsible for choosing configurations and content appropriate to the
Learners they support. \progname{} does not independently determine the
suitability of a learning activity for an individual Learner.

\paragraph{Supported Device and Network Responsibilities}

The device provides the input and display capabilities through which users
interact with \progname{}. Network connectivity supports communication with the
system and synchronization of Learner information.

\paragraph{External Email-Delivery Service Responsibilities}

An external email-delivery service supports the account-verification
functionality specified in \ref{FR-CTM-3}. The service is responsible for
delivering verification messages requested by \progname{}, while \progname{}
remains responsible for managing account verification. No specific email
provider is prescribed.

\paragraph{\progname{} Responsibilities}

\progname{} is responsible for receiving and validating user inputs, managing
CTM accounts and Learner profiles, maintaining device pairings, and enforcing
access permissions.

During gameplay, the system is responsible for presenting authorized Games with
the appropriate Learner Game Configuration or assigned Template. It also
provides feedback, manages rewards and cosmetic items, and records Game Session
information.

For CTMs, \progname{} provides access to permitted management functions and
representations of Learner performance information. It maintains the
relationships between Learners, CTMs, Games, configurations, and session
records.

\progname{} is also responsible for temporarily retaining unsynchronized Game
Session results when network connectivity is unavailable, as specified in
\ref{FR-DATA-8}.

The system requests delivery of account-verification messages through the
external email-delivery service as part of the CTM registration process.

\progname{} is an educational support system rather than a clinical or
safety-critical application. Its intended use involves providing opportunities
for practice and presenting information about performance within the Games.

Although it is not a clinical system, \progname{} manages information about
potentially vulnerable users. Appropriate access controls and handling of
Learner information are therefore important to its operation.

The external email-delivery service supports account verification through
communication beyond \progname{}'s application boundary. This does not prescribe
a particular email provider or implementation technology.

\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

\progname{} has two primary user groups: Learners and Care Team Members (CTMs).
Each group has different responsibilities, technical abilities, and interaction
needs.

\paragraph{Learners}

Learners are autistic individuals who use \progname{} to participate in Games
and practise selected skills. The initial target population identified by the
project is children aged eight to ten, although suitability depends on individual
abilities and support needs rather than age alone.

Autistic individuals have varied communication characteristics and may be
more or less sensitive to sensory input~\citep{nimhAutism2025}. For design
purposes, the project also assumes that Learners may vary in attention, reading
skills, motor coordination, and experience with digital devices and Games.

The intended Learner should be able to make intentional selections using a
supported interaction method, independently or with assistance. Independent
reading, spoken communication, and advanced technical knowledge are not assumed.

Learners are expected to interact primarily with the Game Suite. Their tasks
include selecting authorized Games, responding to instructions, controlling
gameplay, and interacting with available rewards or cosmetic items. They are not
expected to understand account management, permission structures, or performance
analytics.

\paragraph{Care Team Members (CTMs)}

CTMs include parents, guardians, caregivers, educators, and other individuals
involved in supporting a Learner's activities.

CTMs are expected to have basic digital literacy, including the ability to
navigate an application, create or access an account, enter information, and
manage settings. Programming experience or knowledge of software development is
not required.

CTMs are expected to have sufficient familiarity with the Learner's needs and
preferences to select appropriate Games, adjust configurations, and interpret
recorded performance information.

CTMs may have different levels of authority within a Learner's care team. The
Primary CTM manages other members' permissions, while other CTMs can access
functions according to the permissions assigned to them.

\paragraph{Domain Experts}

Domain Experts, such as special-education professionals and autism researchers,
are stakeholders who may provide guidance on the appropriateness of learning
activities and the interpretation of results. They are not defined as a separate
application user role in the current functional requirements.

\subsection{System Constraints}

The following constraints limit the operating environment and design choices for
\progname{}. They are distinguished from functional and nonfunctional
requirements, which describe the system's behavior and quality attributes in
later sections.

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{SC1 --- Supported Operating Platforms}]\hypertarget{SC1}{}
\progname{} is intended to support tablet applications on iPadOS and Android, as
well as access through a web browser. Support for additional desktop operating
systems, including Windows and macOS, may be considered depending on
implementation and testing feasibility. Linux support is not a priority for the
initial release.
\item[\textbf{SC2 --- Supported Input Hardware}]\hypertarget{SC2}{}
The initial system is intended to support touch input on compatible tablets.
Additional input methods, including keyboard and pointer interaction, may be
supported where appropriate for the selected deployment platforms.
\item[\textbf{SC3 --- Networked Account and Data Management}]\hypertarget{SC3}{}\hfill\\
Network connectivity is required for operations involving shared account
information, care team membership, and synchronization of Learner data.
\ref{FR-DATA-8} specifies temporary retention of unsynchronized Game Session
results but does not establish full offline operation.
\end{description}

\section{User Personas}

\subsection{Persona 1}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Layla
\item Description: Layla is a 9-year-old autistic learner who enjoys speaking her mind. Layla is a strong academic student that excels in mathematics and reading. Layla also struggles with social interactions and has difficulty understanding social cues which hamper her ability to make friends and socialize with peers or the public. Layla can be easily overstimulated by loud noises, bright lights or fast movements.
\item Goals: Layla wants to make friends easier and conversate without being scared or shy. She also wants to express herself and understand other people's expressions.
\item Frustrations: Layla finds friends hard to make. She feels misunderstood when she expresses herself. Games can often contain: loud noises, bright lights and cluttered screens that can be overwhelming.
\item Technical Ability: Layla is a tech-savvy child that enjoys playing video games on her laptop. She is comfortable with basic computer functions such as dragging and dropping, clicking and typing.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Persona 2}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Adam
\item Description: Adam is a 6-year-old autistic learner who enjoys being social and chatty with his friends. Adam struggles with executive functioning and has difficulty focusing on academic tasks for extended periods of time, especially in mathematics and reading. Adam can be quickly understimulated by repetitive tasks and become bored.
\item Goals: Learn math without being bored. Find reading easy. Focus on academic activities for longer. 
\item Frustrations: Adam thinks math is boring and reading is hard, and that both take too long without any stimulation. Educational games lack rewards or any incentive to complete.
\item Technical Ability: Adam does not have access to a laptop, but is comfortable on touch screen devices such as his tablet. He does not like flat-looking designs with nothing particularly engaging happening. 
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Persona 3}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Noah
\item Description: Noah is a 65-year-old licensed educator at an Autism Learning Center. Noah teaches classrooms of 10 autistic learners at a time, often in the 4-10 year old range. Noah also teaches autistic learners 1-on-1 across a range of skills including social skills, communication, mathematics and reading. Noah uses physical materials either hand-crafted or externally purchased to facilitate learning activities with his students.
\item Goals: Noah wants to track the progress of his diverse set of students individually across different skills. Noah wants a more malleable method of tailoring his activities for each student's preferences.
\item Frustrations: Noah finds that having several students can strain the time available to analyze each one's specific progress, whether they have made any and to what extent. Noah likes the physical materials employed in his activities, but they are not malleable enough to be personalized for each student's experience.
\item Technical Ability: Noah's formative years in the world of teaching autistic learners preceded widespread technology. Therefore, Noah is able to use tablets and computers at a basic level, but it may be confusing with more complicated functionality or presentation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Persona 4}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name: Sara
\item Description: Sara is a 32-year-old mother of a 6-year-old autistic child. Sara's job in the engineering industry enables her to quickly adopt new technologies and use them proficiently. She is experienced at reading and analyzing data. However, Sara's knowledge in the field of autism is limited.
\item Goals: Sara wants to aid her child's learning path. Sara knows her child's preferences and habits well, and wants to create a curated experience for her child based on this knowledge. 
\item Frustrations: Sara is unsure which activities are suitable for the skill development of autistic children. When she does find a pre-packaged activity, her child tends to not respond well because the experience is not personalized.
\item Technical Ability: Sara is proficient at hugely different devices and technologies. She is comfortable with laptops, tablets and reading visualized or numerical data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Use Cases}

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_Label1}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_Label1}: Play a game}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

Autistic Learner

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item A CTM has authorized that the Learner can play this game.
\item A CTM has either approved default settings or configured personalized settings for this game for the learner.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item System's game suite displays games Learner can play.
\item Learner selects a specific game from their game suite.
\item System loads the selected game with the correct settings. 
\item Learner chooses to start the game when they are ready.
\item System continues presenting tasks completed by the Learner.
\item After the final task is completed, System loads the final completion screen.
\item System saves the Learner's performance data from the session into the correct database(s).
\item Learner claims their in-game reward.
\item Learner exits the game.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item 1.1: System displays that the Learner cannot play any games at the moment.

\item 2.1: The system fails to load the selected game with the correct setting.

2.2: CTM or Learner aborts the game.

\item 3.1: Learner chooses to return to game suite screen.

\item 4.1: Learner finds instruction unclear. 

4.2: Learner wants a Guide for this instruction.

4.3: System loads Guide.

4.4: Learner closes Guide.

\item 6.1: System does not find a stable connection to the database(s).

6.2: System queues the data for future syncing.

\item 8.1: Learner or CTM start the game again.

\end{itemize}

\refstepcounter{ucnum} \label{UC_Label2}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_Label2}: Adding a Learner}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

CTM

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The CTM is logged into their account.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item CTM chooses to Add Learner.
\item System loads the configuration page for a new learner's information.
\item CTM inputs Learner information.
\item CTM chooses which games this Learner can play, with configurable per-game settings.
\item System displays a special Learner code for pairing purposes.
\item The special code is entered correctly by an actor on the Learner's device(s).
\item System saves the Learner's information to the appropriate tables in the database.
\item System visually confirms successful pairing on the CTM account (Learner added) and the Learner's device(s).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item 1.1: CTM chooses to transfer Learner profile to their account from another CTM.

\section{Safety Requirements}

The following were generated from the hazard analysis:

\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{SR-1}] The system shall enforce password strength on creation of account. 
    \item[\textbf{SR-2.}] The system shall end a CTM's session after a period of inactivity.
    \item[\textbf{SR-3.}] The system shall require a CTM to re-enter their password before deleting a Learner profile, deleting their account, transferring ownership of a Learner profile or removing a CTM from a care team.
    \item[\textbf{SR-4.}] Default stimulation level, volume and celebration intensity shall be set to low values until a CTM changes them.
    \item[\textbf{SR-5.}] No game audio or animation shall begin before the Learner gives the cue to start.
    \item[\textbf{SR-6.}] Games shall not contain content that flashes more than three times in any one-second period (following WCAG 2.x Success Criterion 2.3.1).
    \item[\textbf{SR-7.}] Error messages shown to a Learner shall be calm and short, shall not use alarming sounds or visuals, and shall offer a single action to return to the game suite.
    \item[\textbf{SR-8.}] Sessions that end through a crash, abort or lost connection shall be recorded as incomplete and shall not be counted in a Learner's performance averages.
    \item[\textbf{SR-9.}] A loss of connection shall not interrupt a game session in progress or prevent a Learner from playing authorized games with settings already stored on the device.
    \item[\textbf{SR-10.}] Every game task shall be completable with either touch or mouse input.
    \item[\textbf{SR-11.}] Pause, restart and exit controls shall be visible and responsive at all times while a game is being played.
    \item[\textbf{SR-12.}] Cosmetic items hidden by a CTM shall be hidden from the items screen and shall not be purchasable or equippable by the Learner.
    \item[\textbf{SR-13.}] The system shall confirm a save of settings, templates or equipped items only after the write has been verified; if saving fails, the previous values shall be kept and the CTM or Learner informed.
    \item[\textbf{SR-14.}] Before a template is assigned, un-assigned or deleted, the system shall show the affected Learners and what will be replaced, require confirmation, and show a preview of the template.
    \item[\textbf{SR-15.}] Imported files shall be treated as untrusted data and shall never be executed.
    \item[\textbf{SR-16.}] An export of Learner data shall contain every record for the selected Learner, game and date range, and shall report a record count that matches the dashboard for the same selection.
    \item[\textbf{SR-17.}] The system shall automatically back up stored data regularly and shall support restoring from a backup.
    \item[\textbf{SR-18.}] The system shall verify that deletion of a Learner's data has completed and shall remove the data from backups within a fixed retention period (defined in the SRS Appendix of auxiliary constants).
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Requirements}

\subsection{Major Data Entities}

\begin{itemize}
\item Learner: The profile of a Learner and their paired devices.
\item Device: information pertaining to a Learner's paired devices.
\item CTM: The registered CTM account with login credentials and template folders.
\item Care Team Membership: Each link between a CTM and a Learner, the permissions associated and whether they're the primary CTM.
\item Game: A game object with its information and default settings.
\item Game Session: A play-through session of a Learner on a specific game with scores and other relevant statistics.
\item Learner Game Configuration: The tailored per-game configuration for a Learner, including personalized settings, assigned templates and authorizations.
\item Template: A CTM-made variation of an existing game with customized content and/or settings.

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}
\label{7.3: Privacy and Data Retention}

Learner, sessions and CTM data are stored on a central server in Canada. A learner's paired devices only keep what is required to play: learner game configurations, learner inventory and local session data waiting to sync.

Deleted Learner profiles and CTM accounts enter a 30-day grace period after which they are permenantly deleted including all backups. The data is no longer scheduled for deletion if the account or profile is restored within the 30-day grace period. Deleting a Learner profile entails the unlinking of any paired devices and all profile access to other CTMs. Before deleting a Learner profle, the primary CTM can download the Learner data.

The system collects minimal info about Learners because they have no login credentials nor do they own a traditional account. The system has no advertising or third-party tracking of Learners. The primary CTM (eg. a parent) controls the extent of permissions and access of other CTM's regarding the Learner's configurations and data. Any changes by a CTM to a Learner's settings is logged clearly for other CTMs to see.

When creating a Learner profile, the creator confirms they are the Learner's legal guardian or have permission from the guardian to create/manage this profile. CTMs must agree to a list of terms \& conditions and privacy policies upon account registration. A CTM joining a Learner's care team requires an intentional invite from the primary CTM. 

All data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Passwords are stored as securely hashed passwords rather than a readable form. Any session data being collected or queued for syncing on a paired device is kept in protected app storage until syncing, then deleted off the local storage.

The system shall comply with Canada's federal PIPEDA law and the EU's GDPR law. Additionally, the system shall meet the conditions of app store rules for children's apps regarding ads, analytics and external links.

For functional requirements regarding CTMs, we are allowing CTM's to delete and restore their accounts or Learners for which they are authorized to do so to comply with industry standard privacy policies for handling user data. 

For functional requirements regarding Learner profiles, we've decided to not give the Learner a typical login-style account. This is because we do not want the Learner, who may be young or possess untrained memory skills, to remember their login every time. Therefore, we have created a device pairing system for the Learner to play on their authorized device(s) when setting up, not requiring any further admin steps after that.

For the decision to make a primary CTM, we wanted to avoid conflicting decisions in the Care Team to affect the Learner. We also wanted to give an assured way for legal guardians to be comfortable with allowing a specialist, educator or therapist to adjust settings without feeling powerless to revert their changes or remove them from handling the Learner profile altogether. In this manner, the legal guardian has the right to remove other CTMs who are no longer affiliated with the Learner's learning path, instead inviting a new CTM for example. The granularity of the permissions the primary CTM can invoke on each CTM should further comfort a legal guardian in their control over who can affect the Learner's experience.

Regarding the extensive personalization and template feature: autistic learners specifically are more easily prone to either understimulation or overstimulation. In that case, we should not adopt a one-size-fits-all gameplay experience as a typical educational game suite would, but rather allow the CTMs that are most knowledgeable about the Learner to have stronger capability to tailor the experience for the Learner.

THe gameplay is designed to be low-pressure with the ability to pause, abort and find help all afforded to the Learner by default. This further emphasizes the experience of the Learner and the self-applied guardrails we've employed to not cause a Learner any unintended distress. The Learner's data section regarding these games is also crucial for the CTMs to assess the Learner's current skillset and for progress tracking. The reward system is another method by which we plan to boost the Learner's engagement and positive reinforcement instead of repetitive educational gameplay they may easily be bored by.\\

The data requirements follow suit to what the functional requirements say about the system. The major data entities can be seen as nouns in the functional requirements for the most part. The Learner Game Configuration is a table designed to hold the Learner's configured settings for each game by the CTM's authorization. Device and Access Code are two entities that operate together in the case of the Learner's device pairing, though the Access Code table doubles as an invite system for CTMs to join a care team. 

The data system is decomposed in such a way that objects created from these entities do not destroy other objects upon deletion. For example, despite the CTM account 'owning' the Learner profile, deleting the CTM account should not also delete a Learner's profile. Even with data entities such as Learner Inventory; the aim is to not create objects that are inefficient for a database to query what it requires. It is more efficient to have a Learner Inventory entity than to have either the Learner store their cosmetic items in the same table, whether they are equipped or not etc.\\

There are no AI requirements because we are not employing a machine learning model in Spectropica.

\section{Unlikely Changes}

The following assumptions are considered unlikely to change during the
development of \progname{}. They define stable characteristics that the system's
design can rely on. Each assumption is supported by the system's intended
purpose, user responsibilities, or existing requirements.

\noindent \begin{itemize}

\newcounter{ulcnum}

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_Roles}:] \textbf{Distinction Between Learners and CTMs}

\progname{} will continue to distinguish between Learners, who participate in
Games, and Care Team Members (CTMs), who manage and support their learning
activities. The specific functions available to each group may evolve, but their
primary responsibilities are expected to remain separate.

Maintaining this distinction allows Learners to focus on participating in Games
without being responsible for administrative tasks, while CTMs retain
responsibility for managing activities and interpreting results.

\textbf{Traceability:} Learner (Table~\ref{tab:Learner}), CTM
(Table~\ref{tab:CTM}).

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_LearnerProfiles}:] \textbf{Individual Learner Profiles}

Each Learner will continue to have an identifiable profile associated with their
Games, configurations, and Game Session records. This relationship is
fundamental to providing individualized activities and maintaining a history of
performance.

Maintaining individual profiles also allows \progname{} to associate the correct
configurations and records with a Learner when they use different paired devices
or receive support from multiple CTMs.

\textbf{Traceability:} Learner (Table~\ref{tab:Learner}), Learner Game
Configuration (Table~\ref{tab:LearnerGameConfig}), Game Session
(Table~\ref{tab:GameSession}); \ref{FR-LRN-1}, \ref{FR-DATA-7}.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_CareTeamPermissions}:] \textbf{Care Team Membership and
Permissions}

\progname{} will continue to associate CTMs with Learners through care team
memberships. Each active Learner profile will have a Primary CTM responsible for
managing the permissions of other CTMs. This structure establishes clear
responsibility for access management when multiple individuals support the same
Learner.

Although the available permissions may change, the distinction between
authorized and unauthorized actions is expected to remain. The identity of the
Primary CTM may change through the ownership-transfer process without changing
the purpose of the role.

\textbf{Traceability:} CTM (Table~\ref{tab:CTM}), Care Team Membership
(Table~\ref{tab:Care Team Membership}); \ref{FR-LRN-2}, \ref{FR-TEAM-4},
\ref{FR-TEAM-6}, \ref{FR-TEAM-7}.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_Personalization}:] \textbf{CTM-Controlled Personalization}

CTMs will continue to manage Learner-specific Game settings and authorizations.
The system is expected to preserve the principle that personalized activities
are configured by authorized CTMs rather than automatically prescribed based on
assumptions about a Learner's developmental needs.

This approach maintains CTM responsibility for selecting activities appropriate
to individual Learners, even if the range of available personalization settings
expands.

\textbf{Traceability:} Learner Game Configuration
(Table~\ref{tab:LearnerGameConfig}), Template (Table~\ref{tab:Template});
\ref{FR-PERS-1} through \ref{FR-PERS-4}.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_Games}:] \textbf{Games as the Primary Learning Experience}

Games will remain the central activities through which Learners interact with
\progname{}. The content and number of Games may change, but their role as the
primary means of providing practice opportunities is expected to remain.

Replacing Games with a different primary learning format would fundamentally
change \progname{}'s intended purpose rather than simply extend its
functionality.

\textbf{Traceability:} Game (Table~\ref{tab:Game}), Game Session
(Table~\ref{tab:GameSession}).

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_PerformanceRecords}:] \textbf{Game-Specific Performance Records}

\progname{} will continue to record performance information associated with
individual Learners and Game Sessions. The metrics and methods of presentation
may evolve, but the relationship between a Learner, a Game, and its recorded
session results is expected to remain.

Maintaining these relationships allows CTMs to review individual Game histories
and interpret recorded results in the context of the activities completed.

\textbf{Traceability:} Learner (Table~\ref{tab:Learner}), Game
(Table~\ref{tab:Game}), Game Session (Table~\ref{tab:GameSession});
\ref{FR-DATA-2}, \ref{FR-DATA-4}, \ref{FR-DATA-7}.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_Participation}:] \textbf{Learner Control Over Participation}

Learners will continue to have the ability to pause, restart, or end an ongoing
Game. These controls allow Learners to stop or repeat an activity according to
their needs and preferences, making them an important part of the intended
gameplay experience.

Removing these controls would reduce the flexibility available to Learners
during participation and would conflict with the existing gameplay requirements.

\textbf{Traceability:} \ref{FR-PLAY-5}, \ref{FR-PLAY-6}, \ref{FR-PLAY-7}.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\arabic{ulcnum}
  \label{ULC_NonClinicalPurpose}:] \textbf{Non-Clinical Purpose}

\progname{} will remain an educational support application rather than a
clinical assessment or diagnostic tool. Performance information will continue to
describe outcomes within Games rather than establish broader developmental
conclusions.

Changing this purpose would require a substantial reconsideration of the
system's scope and validation requirements.

\textbf{Traceability:} Game (Table~\ref{tab:Game}), Game Session
(Table~\ref{tab:GameSession}).

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Appendix E: Reflection}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{What went well during the development of the SRS?}

One aspect that went well was our team's ability to build on our initial project
proposal and develop a more detailed understanding of what \progname{} needs to
accomplish. As we worked through the Software Requirements Specification (SRS),
we were able to break down our overall project goals into more specific system
functionalities and identify how different parts of the application would
interact. Having a shared understanding of our project's purpose helped us
organize the requirements and maintain consistency between sections. We also
benefited from being able to discuss different ideas as a team and refine our
decisions as the project became more clearly defined.

\item \textbf{What challenges did you experience, and how did you resolve them?}

One of the main challenges we experienced was distinguishing between high-level
project goals and specific system requirements. Initially, it was difficult to
determine how much detail belonged in each section and how to describe what the
system needed to accomplish without prescribing exactly how it would be
implemented.

Another challenge was ensuring that the requirements remained consistent across
different sections of the SRS, especially as our understanding of the project
evolved. We addressed these challenges by referring back to the SRS template,
reviewing the requirements as a team, and comparing them against our overall
project objectives. This helped us make our requirements more specific while
ensuring they continued to support the intended purpose of \progname{}.

\item \textbf{How many requirements were inspired by discussions with clients,
stakeholders, or potential users?}

Approximately ten of our requirements were influenced by discussions with our
project supervisor, who is our domain expert in psychology and autism. These
discussions helped us better understand the different needs and abilities of
autistic Learners, particularly the importance of personalization,
accessibility, and flexibility during gameplay. This influenced requirements
related to Game customization, adjustable settings, clear instructions, and
allowing Learners to control their participation. The discussions helped us
ensure that our requirements considered the needs of our intended users rather
than focusing solely on technical functionality.

\item \textbf{Which courses helped prepare your team for this capstone
deliverable?}

SFWRENG 3RA3, our Software Requirements course, was especially useful for
developing this deliverable. The course introduced us to the process of
identifying, organizing, and documenting software requirements, which directly
applied to writing our SRS. In particular, it helped us understand the
distinction between functional and nonfunctional requirements, the importance of
clear and unambiguous wording, and how to structure requirements so that they
can be understood and evaluated.

The knowledge gained from this course provided a strong foundation for
translating our project idea into a more formal specification.
